Tested against four hand-built schedules with known answers (table, checklist, kitchen grid, dense two-column spec) and a real care-home cleaning checklist pulled off the web. On anything a person can comfortably read, it pulls out 19 of every 20 tasks or better, gets the cleaning frequency right about nine times in ten, and - the number that matters most for trust - it never fabricates a task that isn't on the sheet (100% precision across every single test). When it's unsure, it leaves it out rather than guessing.

02 How much you can ruin the photo before it gives up

I took one clean schedule and battered it 22 ways - rotation, blur, darkness, glare, compression, noise, skew, downscaling, and nasty combinations - then ran each through the real app. The headline: it is very tolerant of bad lighting and bad cameras, and its weak spot is tilt and heavy blur. Every failure is graceful: a clear "couldn't read this, try a clearer photo" message, never wrong data.

It handles a dim utility room, glare, faded print, brutal JPEG compression, speckled noise, a blocked corner, an angled shot, and even a photo taken fully sideways. It falls over on a slight hand-held tilt, heavy blur, or several bad things stacked together.

03 The bug the stress test caught

Found + fixed

High-resolution photos were being fed into the reader's blind spot.

The app shrank every uploaded image to a maximum of 3000px before reading it. It turns out the text-recognition engine silently drops entire tables at that exact size - it read the title and footer but zero of the 11 tasks. And every modern phone shoots at 3000-4000px, so a cleaner's photo landed right in that blind spot. Measured: 0 of 11 tasks at 3000px, 11 of 11 at 2000px and below. I lowered the cap to 2000px, which is the engine's sweet spot. Without the stress test this would have quietly failed for real users on good photos.

04 The one real weakness - and how to close it

Recommendation

A slightly tilted photo is the most likely real-world failure.

A photo held even 3 to 15 degrees off level - which is how most people snap a quick handheld picture - is where accuracy drops or fails, while a photo taken fully sideways (90 deg) reads perfectly. That's a quirk of the text engine: it copes with square rotations but not small skews. One striking case: a dim, slightly-tilted, compressed photo that is perfectly readable to a human came back empty.

Two ways to close it, in order of value: (1) auto-straighten the photo before reading it (deskew) - this directly fixes the single most common real failure; (2) offer a local vision model for images instead of text-recognition - it would read the tilted, dim, and blurred photos a human can still read. The switch to plug one in already exists in the code. Neither is required to ship; both raise the floor on messy real-world photos.

05 On phones and tablets

Swept every screen as both an admin and a cleaner, at four sizes - small phone (360px), standard phone (390px), tablet portrait (768px) and landscape (1024px): 27 screens, zero horizontal overflow, zero console errors. The structure is solid. I found and fixed six real layout issues, the most important being on the cleaner's phone screens.

The room a cleaner actually works from: the assignee name was cut off at the edge ("Sarah Johnso..."). Now the details wrap cleanly and nothing is clipped.

Every dashboard metric label was truncated ("Team M...", "Total Ro..."). Now they read in full and the four cards line up.

The six fixes

  • Cleaner room screen (phone): room details no longer clip off the edge - they wrap.
  • Admin dashboard (phone): metric labels read in full instead of "Team M..."; cards aligned.
  • Users table (phone): the Edit / Block buttons were unreachable off-screen - now reachable, and "Add User" no longer bleeds off the edge.
  • Schedule + Audit (phone): the "Edit Mode" and "Export CSV" buttons no longer break onto two ragged lines.

A handful of lower-priority cosmetic notes remain (a couple of tablet-landscape cards sit wide with empty space, and the cleaner portal mixes blue and green accents) - none block use; happy to polish on request.

06 Bottom line

Ship-ready, with one upgrade path

What you can offer today: a cleaner photographs any schedule and gets 19-of-20 tasks or better back, with correct frequencies most of the time and zero made-up tasks. On a clear photo it's near-perfect; on a bad one it tells them to retake rather than saving something wrong.

The one thing worth doing next: auto-straighten photos before reading them, to catch the slight-tilt case that's the most common real-world miss. Everything verified against a live local model and a running app - no OpenAI key, no per-photo cost, care-home data never leaves the machine.
